# Research: Which alternative cipher families should Phase 5 test first?

**Date:** 2026-09-21 · **Decision this feeds:** the first non-Enigma model-selection experiment for the September 1941 Batch C corpus · **Out of scope:** claiming a cipher identification or plaintext from ciphertext-only summary statistics

## Facts

- CryptoCellar's current unbroken list names BYQMZ, FKQLZ, and XFEDT in September Batch C. QTXMA and SZAEJ are absent, but the accessible source does not explain their disposition. — [CryptoCellar unbroken-message list](https://www.cryptocellar.org/bgac/1941-msg-list-unbroken.html), status 2026-09-19; accessed 2026-09-21
- A declassified Friedman lecture identifies double transposition as another German Army cipher and describes its World War II cryptanalysis. — [The Friedman Lectures](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/lectures-speeches/FOLDER_166/41759739079888.pdf), declassified historical lecture; accessed 2026-09-21
- Friedman's treatment of periodic polyalphabetic systems explains causal repetitions when identical plaintext material is enciphered under the same cipher-alphabet positions, and contrasts these with random coincidences. — [Military Cryptanalysis, Part II](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/publications/FOLDER_508/41784299082338.pdf), 1938/1941 editions; accessed 2026-09-21
- The NSA's survey of German cipher machines documents Enigma, T-52 cipher teleprinters, and SZ-40/SZ-42 teleprinter attachments as German World War II systems. — [German Cipher Machines of World War II](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/about/cryptologic-heritage/historical-figures-publications/publications/wwii/german_cipher.pdf), 2014; accessed 2026-09-21
- The NSA NARA release catalog includes captured records titled “German Division Hand Key” and “German 3 Letter Code Tables,” both dated 1940. — [NSA World War II NARA Releases](https://www.nsa.gov/History/Cryptologic-History/NARA-Releases/), living catalog; accessed 2026-09-21
- The corrected 4,000-trial Phase 5 run routes QTXMA to the frequency-preserving/manual branch: IC 0.057729 with uniform-null upper-tail `p = 1/4001`. Against frequency-preserving permutations, its scan-corrected lag (`p = 0.0875`), candidate-period (`p = 0.1647`), and repeated-trigram (`p = 0.0820`) tests are not significant at `alpha = 0.01`. The other four messages remain compatible with the uniform-random control under the configured tests. — [generated Phase 5 artifact](../artifacts/phase5-model-triage.json), 2026-09-21

## Inference

- A high monographic index of coincidence should route a message toward frequency-preserving systems, especially transposition, but cannot distinguish transposition from monoalphabetic substitution.
- A random-like monographic distribution does not favor Enigma over a cipher teleprinter or a strong superencipherment. Traffic format, procedure, cribs, or key recovery must do that work.
- Period scans and lag scans must be calibrated against the maximum statistic from each frequency-preserving permutation trial; treating every tested period or lag as an independent uncorrected result, or comparing high-IC ciphertext to uniform A-Z trials, manufactures false positives.
- The five messages should be assessed separately before pooling. Phase 4 already showed that optimizing a shared model can overfit the visible messages and fail on held-out traffic.
- QTXMA is the evidence-backed first target for a frequency-preserving family experiment. The corrected conditional tests provide no separate evidence for periodic, lag, or repeated-block structure.

## Speculation

- QTXMA's visibly repetitive ciphertext may belong to a frequency-preserving manual system, a weak superencipherment, or a non-cryptographic/retransmission artifact. No source currently establishes which.
- The two trigrams on each form may be procedural carryover rather than proof that every body was generated by Enigma. This is a hypothesis to test, not a historical fact.

## Unresolved

- The original grouping, spacing, typography, and any operator corrections need to be re-extracted from the message-form facsimiles. Normalized A-Z strings discard evidence useful for code and transposition identification.
- The reason QTXMA and SZAEJ disappeared from the current unbroken list needs an archival or curator answer.
- Teleprinter-family plausibility cannot be ranked responsibly until the intercept medium and form-transcription path are documented.
- A frequency-preserving signal still requires a discriminating experiment: transposition search with held-out scoring, substitution controls, and exact preservation of message boundaries.

## Recommendation

Run deterministic, per-message structural triage first. Route any strong frequency-preserving outlier to a preregistered double-transposition experiment; route credible periodic outliers to repeating-key tests; leave random-like messages in a combined rotor/additive-machine branch until metadata can separate them.

Main risk: short ciphertexts create unstable tail statistics and can make an ordinary randomizing cipher look structured.

Strongest case against: the forms and indicator trigrams may already make standard Enigma so much more likely that alternative-family work has low expected value.

Reverse this if: the missing status history for QTXMA/SZAEJ reveals a confirmed solution or reclassification, or the facsimiles show that normalization created the apparent structure.

# Addendum 2026-09-23 — conservation gate

## Cause

The recommendation above routed QTXMA to a double-transposition experiment on a
frequency-preserving signal that was measured only as elevated index of
coincidence. Phases 6 and 7 followed that route, ran preregistered searches with
controls, and recorded refutations. Both were methodologically sound and both
were aimed at a hypothesis that letter conservation had already excluded.

The error was in this document's routing logic, not in the experiments that
obeyed it. Index of coincidence measures how concentrated a letter distribution
is. It is blind to *which* letters carry the concentration. Routing to a
frequency-preserving family requires the second fact and never tested for it.

## Fact

- A transposition permutes plaintext symbols. It cannot create or destroy a
  letter, so the ciphertext letter multiset equals the plaintext letter multiset.
- Unigram frequencies derived from the published 1941 Army bigram counts
  (`data/phase7/BigramFrequency1941.txt`, 17,694 bigrams) give D at 2.90%, U at
  4.47%, F at 3.03%, G at 2.81%, and Y at 0.89%. E leads at 12.91% and X reaches
  6.98%, consistent with Army operator text where X marks punctuation.
- QTXMA contains no D, F, G or U in 155 characters. Its commonest letter is Y at
  12.3%.
- QTXMA's chi-square distance from those reference monograms is 3.384 per
  letter. Shuffles of five published Army plaintexts — true transpositions —
  score 0.174 to 0.678.
- SZAEJ, the other 29 September message, also lacks D, F and U, and scores 2.426
  per letter.

## Inference

- QTXMA is not a transposition of German Army plaintext. This follows from
  conservation and does not depend on any search, scorer or key.
- Its letters are concentrated on a 22-letter alphabet that is not the plaintext
  alphabet, which places a substitution or encoding step between Army plaintext
  and the observed text.
- The shared {D, F, U} absence across exactly the two messages that left the
  unbroken list is suggestive of a shared system, but two messages is not a
  pattern and 51 characters of SZAEJ is thin evidence.

## Method

`frequency_preserving` now requires `ic_elevated` and
`plaintext_unigram_compatible`. The second fails when either the reference
chi-square or the absent-letter surprisal is significant against a
length-matched null at alpha.

The null draws each trial message's letter distribution from a Dirichlet centred
on the reference before drawing letters, because real messages differ from the
pooled corpus by more than sampling noise — each has its own subject, place
names and numerals. Without that term the null is too tight and excludes genuine
transpositions.

The absent-letter test scores total surprisal rather than the joint probability
of the absences. The raw joint product shrinks with message length whether or
not anything is anomalous, so it cannot be compared against a fixed threshold; a
first implementation of this gate did exactly that and wrongly excluded
`1941-09-06-36`, a true transposition.

The gate is a positive-control construct in the Phase 6 and 7 sense. Every
control plaintext in `data/phase5/army-plaintext-controls.json` is shuffled into
a true transposition and must be found compatible. All five pass, worst at
`p = 0.0947` against alpha 0.01. If any failed, the gate would be recorded and
not applied, and every message would keep its pre-gate route.

## Speculation

- A digraphic system would produce a restricted alphabet and an IC near the
  observed 0.0577. QTXMA's length of 155 is odd, which argues against a pure
  bigram cipher and should be checked before any such family is implemented.
- No family has been added to `cipher-families.json` for this route. Doing so
  requires sourced provenance for a specific system, which this addendum does
  not have.

## Unresolved

- What maps Army plaintext onto QTXMA's 22-letter alphabet.
- Whether SZAEJ shares that mechanism or merely resembles it at 51 characters.
- Whether the conservation exclusion would survive a plaintext layer that is not
  German Army text — a codebook output, for instance, would have its own
  monograms and the gate's reference would not apply.

## Recommendation

Do not reopen a transposition search for QTXMA on width or scorer grounds. It is
closed by conservation, and reopening it needs a positive reason to believe the
underlying layer is not German Army plaintext.

Apply the conservation test before any future family-specific search. It is
O(n), it needs no optimizer, and it would have saved two preregistered
experiments and a revision cycle here.

Main risk: the gate's reference is derived from published counts whose source
messages are not enumerated, so it inherits whatever selection produced them.

Strongest case against: the gate tests German Army plaintext specifically, and a
transposition over any other plaintext layer escapes it entirely.

Reverse this if: archival evidence shows QTXMA's body is a superencipherment of
code groups rather than of literal text, in which case the reference
distribution is the wrong one and the exclusion does not apply.

# Addendum 2026-10-20 — trigram repeats (`phase5-trigram-repeat-v1`)

## Cause

Frode Weierud replied on 2026-10-11 to the maintainer's question about the
2005 paper's "differently wired wheels" footnote. He now believes none of
the five Batch C messages with Enigma-type indicators is Enigma, because
their letter frequencies are very uneven and they "have trigram repeats where
none would be expected". The repository had tested letter frequencies for
QTXMA only (the conservation gate above) and had never counted trigram repeats.

## Method

Preregistered in `experiments/phase5-trigram-repeat-v1/config.json`
(`490fb82`) before any statistic was computed on a target. The run was made
from `d01c97d` on a clean tree. It took 99 s, and the result is in
`artifacts/phase5-trigram-repeat-v1.json`.

- **Statistic:** the number of extra occurrences of repeated trigrams,
  summed over trigrams. Windows that contain BYQMZ's uncertain letter are
  skipped.
- **Null:** 20,000 simulated Enigma I ciphertexts per target, at the
  target's own length and with the same masked position. Each enciphers a
  random window of the 858 letters of authentic 1941 Army plaintext in
  `data/historical/keyed-messages.json`, under a fresh random ten-pair key.
- **Threshold:** one-sided p < 0.002, which is 0.01 Bonferroni-corrected
  over five targets.
- **Calibration:** the 13 authentic Enigma ciphertexts are scored against
  their own length-matched nulls. All 13 had to pass for the null to count
  as calibrated.

## Fact

The calibration held. The 13 real Enigma ciphertexts give trigram-repeat
p-values from 0.046 to 1.0, and none is below 0.002.

That control is weaker than its count suggests. Seven of the 13 score
p = 1.0 because they have no repeated trigram. Messages of 18 to 48 letters
almost never have one, so they cannot fail. Only the longest two, No. 25
(214 letters) and No. 233 (177), sit at or above the targets' lengths of
97 to 167 letters. The control would catch a null that grossly
under-produces repeats at those lengths, but not a modest miscalibration.
The null's own construction carries most of the weight: a real Enigma
model, run on real plaintext. (Recorded after code review of PR #41.)

| Target | Letters | Repeats (null median / 97.5%) | p | Army plaintext windows, 2.5–97.5% | IC (null median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QTXMA | 155 | **5** (0 / 3) | **0.0010** | 8–32 | **0.0577** (0.0383), p = 0.00005 |
| SZAEJ | 51 | 0 (0 / 1) | 1.0 | 0–10 | 0.0525 (0.0376), p = 0.017 |
| BYQMZ | 167 | 0 (1 / 3) | 1.0 | 9–34 | 0.0395 (0.0383), p = 0.26 |
| FKQLZ | 107 | 0 (0 / 2) | 1.0 | 1–23 | 0.0326 (0.0383) |
| XFEDT | 97 | 1 (0 / 2) | 0.22 | 0–19 | 0.0329 (0.0382) |

Only QTXMA is flagged. Its repeats are OQXY at positions 16 and 124,
OEMY at 38 and 44, and XYT at 67 and 126. Only 0.1% of the 20,000
simulated Enigma texts had as many. Its IC was not reached by any draw.

Weierud's prediction, as preregistered, was that at least one target would be
flagged. It **held**, but only through QTXMA.

## Inference

- **QTXMA:** on both trigram repeats and IC, it is inconsistent with Enigma
  or any similar rotor machine. That agrees with Weierud and with the
  conservation gate. Its repeat count is also well below every Army-plaintext
  window of its length (5 against a 2.5% point of 8), while its IC sits at
  the low edge of plaintext. So it is neither rotor output nor a cipher that
  keeps plaintext letter patterns, such as monoalphabetic substitution. The
  gate's routing to `non_plaintext_alphabet_substitution` stands.
- **BYQMZ, FKQLZ and SZAEJ:** they have no repeated trigram at all, and
  XFEDT has one. On this statistic all four look exactly like Enigma
  ciphertext. Weierud's trigram observation does not hold for them under this
  definition. Its evidence for "not Enigma" therefore rests on QTXMA alone.
- **SZAEJ:** its IC is high (p = 0.017) but not significant at the
  threshold, and at 51 letters the test has little power.

## Speculation (exploratory, not preregistered)

FKQLZ and XFEDT are *flatter* than Enigma output. Only 14 and 76 of the
20,000 draws had a lower IC, giving lower-tail p of about 0.001 and 0.004;
ties are not separated. Neither message contains a J. With near-uniform
letters, the chance of no J is 0.015 at 107 letters and 0.022 at 97, and
about 0.0003 for both together. Weierud describes the Batch C messages
*without* Enigma-type indicators as a 25-letter system without J and with a
much flatter frequency distribution. These two messages may belong to that
system despite their Enigma-type indicators. A low IC cannot come from
removing J alone, because a uniform 25-letter alphabet gives 0.040. It
points to a cipher that spreads letters more evenly than chance. This
inference was made after looking at the data and has not been tested.

## Ostwald's 2023 note

Weierud attached Olaf Ostwald's working note "Grey Messages from 29 & 30
Sep 1941" (2 August 2023, 9 pages, German, `Grey_Messages_230802.pdf`). It
was received privately and is not in the repository; it was read on
2026-10-20. It treats the five targets as 5 of 15 "grey" texts of Army
Group North (HG Nord), sent on 29 and 30 September 1941. Its ciphertexts
include the first group, so its lengths are 5 more than `corpus.json`'s.
The points that bear on this addendum follow.

**The J column.** Ostwald marks only Grey06 (QTXMA), Grey07 (SZAEJ) and
Grey08 (BYQMZ) as "mit J". Every other grey text is "ohne J", **including
Grey11 (FKQLZ) and Grey12 (XFEDT)** despite their Enigma-type indicators.
He labels FKQLZ's IC (3.27%) "flat", as he labels two of the J-less texts
without Enigma-type indicators. This is an independent reading of the same
pattern the exploratory section above found. It still does not replace a
preregistered test.

**Shared frequencies and operators.** In his header table:
- **Frequencies:** QTXMA and FKQLZ are both on 323 kHz, and SZAEJ and XFEDT
  both on 716 kHz.
- **Operators:** QTXMA, SZAEJ and BYQMZ were sent by an operator he reads as
  "Stor."; `corpus.json` has "Otor. ?". FKQLZ and XFEDT were sent by
  "Fuhrmann". Stor. also sent Grey05, Grey09 and Grey09b, and Fuhrmann also
  sent Grey10, Grey13 and Grey14a.
- **Handwriting:** all five are in the hand he calls "Cäsar", which also
  wrote Grey05 to Grey14a.
- **A link to BYQMZ:** FKQLZ's operator remark "de ugn" names BYQMZ's
  addressee.
- **Addressee reading:** he reads FKQLZ's addressee as "912", where
  `corpus.json` has "g19".

Among the five messages with Enigma-type indicators only, the "with J" /
"without J" split follows the operator: Stor.'s three have J and
Fuhrmann's two do not. It does not follow the operator in general. Stor.'s
other three messages (Grey05, Grey09, Grey09b) have no J either, so having
J is specific to QTXMA, SZAEJ and BYQMZ, not to Stor.

**The quadgram argument.** Ostwald argues that trigram repeats are expected
by chance and only quadgram repeats are telling. His union-bound estimate
puts a trigram repeat at 70.6% of random 160-letter texts; the Poisson value
is about 51%. Quadgram repeats come out at about 2.7%. His experiment with
one million random texts finds at least one quadgram repeat in 2,202 at
length 50, 9,893 at length 100 and 25,381 at length 160, about 1 in 40.

This agrees with the Enigma null above, whose median at 155–167 letters is
0–1 trigram repeats. It also explains why only QTXMA stands out: it has two
quadgram repeats, OQXY at positions 16 and 124 and OEMY at 38 and 44. The
trigram statistic preregistered here did not separate quadgram repeats from
trigram ones.

**SZAEJ's ZAEJ repeat.** With the first group counted, as Ostwald counts it,
the quadgram ZAEJ of the first group SZAEJ recurs in the body group ZAEJV.
In 56 letters a random text has that chance at roughly 0.2% (his
length-50 count). Under the Enigma convention the first group is a
discriminant and is not enciphered, which is why `corpus.json` and the test
above exclude it. Read as part of the ciphertext, it is SZAEJ's only notable
repeat. This was observed after the fact.

The note also substitutes QTXMA's letters by frequency rank, which gives no
readable text. It shows that a Caesar shift plus a columnar transposition
(keyword BERNHARD) is easy to undo, without saying whether that was tried on
the grey texts.

## Unresolved

- How Weierud defines "trigram repeats", and which messages he counted.
- Whether the J-less, flat-IC pattern of FKQLZ and XFEDT matches the
  ten grey texts without Enigma-type indicators, as Ostwald's table
  suggests. Testing that needs their ciphertexts from a public source;
  Ostwald's note transcribes them but was received privately.
- Whether the first group is enciphered in any of the five: that is, whether
  SZAEJ's ZAEJ repeat is a property of the cipher or chance.
- The two metadata readings where Ostwald and `corpus.json` differ (operator
  "Stor." / "Otor. ?", FKQLZ addressee "912" / "g19"). Settling them needs the
  form facsimiles.

## Recommendation

Preregister a lower-tail IC and missing-J test for FKQLZ and XFEDT against
the same Enigma null. Use the ten grey texts without Enigma-type indicators
as a positive control, taking their ciphertexts from CryptoCellar's Batch C
list. Add a quadgram-repeat count, with and without the first group, as a
preregistered secondary statistic for all five targets.

# Addendum 2026-10-21 — J-less, flat signature (`phase5-jless-flat-v1`)

## Cause

The exploratory section of the 2026-10-20 addendum found FKQLZ and XFEDT
flatter than Enigma output and without J. Ostwald's note then marked both
J-less, like the grey texts without Enigma-type indicators. The
recommendation was to measure that signature under a fixed statistic.

## Method

The configuration is `experiments/phase5-jless-flat-v1/config.json`. It was
preregistered at `951342f`, and its control expectations were stated as
lists at `8df46ee`, before any run. The run was made from `46cf831` on a
clean tree and took 16 min of wall time on one core. The result is in
`artifacts/phase5-jless-flat-v1.json`.

**Framing: disclosed post-hoc.** The hypothesis came from these two messages.
The configuration lists every value already seen. This experiment measures
how strong the observation is; it is not an independent confirmation.

- **Primary statistic:** a joint tail. It is the fraction of simulated texts
  that have no more J than the target *and* an IC no higher. The null is
  Enigma I ciphertext of Army plaintext, as in v1, at each target's length:
  200,000 draws per target.
- **Threshold:** p < 0.001, which is 0.01 Bonferroni-corrected over the ten
  looks that found the lead (five messages times two IC tails).
- **Secondary statistics:**
  - quadgram repeats, with and without the first group;
  - lower-tail IC against uniformly random text over the 25 letters other
    than J (200,000 draws).
- **Negative calibration:** the 13 authentic Enigma ciphertexts. None may
  fall below the threshold.
- **Positive control:** the ten grey texts without Enigma-type indicators, in
  `data/phase5/grey-texts-ostwald-2023.json`. They were transcribed from
  Ostwald's note and committed with attribution on the maintainer's decision.
  Each text's length and IC equal the note's printed values. The preregistered
  expectation was that the four long, flat texts (CSBTR, BCSSF, AWMRI, YDFZS)
  fall below the threshold and the four rough or higher-IC texts (FTBCO,
  AOKAX, XDPAK, SDPLM) do not.

## Observed

**Calibration held.** The 13 authentic Enigma ciphertexts give joint p from
0.081 to 0.93.

**Positive control met its expectation exactly.** Joint p against
length-matched Enigma nulls of 20,000 draws:

| Grey text | Letters | IC | Joint p | Below 0.001 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CSBTR | 111 | 0.0333 | 0.00005 | yes (expected) |
| BCSSF | 93 | 0.0339 | 0.0002 | yes (expected) |
| AWMRI | 83 | 0.0335 | 0.0003 | yes (expected) |
| YDFZS | 81 | 0.0293 | 0.00005 | yes (expected) |
| FTBCO | 105 | 0.0379 | 0.0027 | no (expected) |
| AOKAX | 102 | 0.0485 | 0.015 | no (expected) |
| XDPAK | 103 | 0.0478 | 0.016 | no (expected) |
| SDPLM | 49 | 0.0493 | 0.13 | no (expected) |
| SSRAC | 38 | 0.0213 | 0.00015 | yes (no expectation) |
| KHZMY | 29 | 0.0320 | 0.074 | no (no expectation) |

**Targets** (Enigma null: 200,000 draws each):

| Target | Letters | J | IC | Joint p | p, no J | p, IC lower | p, IC lower vs uniform 25 letters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FKQLZ** | 107 | 0 | 0.0326 | **0.000005** (no draw) | 0.013 | 0.0012 | 0.000005 (no draw) |
| **XFEDT** | 97 | 0 | 0.0329 | **0.000015** (2 draws) | 0.019 | 0.0072 | 0.0003 |

Both targets are flagged, so the preregistered prediction **held**.

**The quadgram secondary statistic carries no information here.** Neither
target, nor any of the ten grey texts, has a repeated quadgram, with or
without the first group, so every quadgram p-value is 1.0.

## Inference

- **Signature:** FKQLZ and XFEDT carry the J-less, flat signature of the grey
  system's flat texts. Their ICs, 3.26% and 3.29%, sit inside the range of
  the four flat long grey texts (2.93–3.39%). Enigma output of Army plaintext
  essentially never shows this signature at their lengths. The test is
  post-hoc, so this is the strength of an observation already made, not an
  independent confirmation.
- **Flatter than random:** both are flatter than uniformly random 25-letter
  text (p ≈ 0.000005 and 0.0003). Removing J cannot explain that, because a
  uniform 25-letter alphabet gives IC about 0.040. Something in the system
  spreads letters more evenly than chance.
- **The grey texts are not one homogeneous population on this statistic.**
  Four long texts are flat, while AOKAX, XDPAK and SDPLM are rough. The
  signature marks the flat subgroup, not every J-less text.
- **Consequence for Phase 1:** every BYQMZ sweep accepted a candidate only if
  *both* companions, FKQLZ and XFEDT, confirmed it at z ≥ 6 under the shared
  daily key. If those two are not Enigma, a true BYQMZ key could never have
  been companion-confirmed, and the decision step of those sweeps had zero
  power whatever the planted-key controls measured. The planted controls gave
  every draw Enigma companions.
  - What survives is detection, not confirmation. In every sweep the top
    BYQMZ score sat at the expected noise maximum, so no candidate stood out
    on BYQMZ's own letters.
  - The odds multipliers stated for the BYQMZ nulls rest on confirmed power
    and must be restated as detection-only. Detection was measured
    separately: 88.8% clean and about 40% with one indel on the easy
    plaintext, lower on typical plaintext.

## Speculation

A system with no J and flatter-than-random letters is what a homophonic
substitution, or a code or bigram system that balances letter use, would
produce. Ostwald's note also tests a Caesar shift with a columnar
transposition, but that would keep the plaintext's unevenness, so it does not
fit these two. None of this has been tested.

## Unresolved

- Whether BYQMZ (it has J, an Enigma-like IC of 0.0395 and no repeats)
  belongs with QTXMA and SZAEJ, which are with-J and rough, or is the one
  genuine Enigma message.
- Whether the six-letter Enigma-type indicators of FKQLZ and XFEDT are
  genuine.
- The flat-system texts' own structure: period, digraphs, and contact
  analysis across the four flat grey texts and the two targets.

## Recommendation

1. Restate the Phase 1 BYQMZ conclusions on detection alone. Recompute, from
   the existing artifacts, whether any retained candidate exceeds the noise
   maximum on BYQMZ by itself, which needs no new sweep. Withdraw the
   confirmation-based odds multipliers.
2. Stop treating FKQLZ and XFEDT as Enigma companions in any new Enigma work.
3. Preregister a structural study of the flat J-less group, the four flat
   grey texts plus FKQLZ and XFEDT: periodicity, digraph and contact
   statistics, against homophonic and bigram-system nulls.
