# Research: Which alternative cipher families should Phase 5 test first?

**Date:** 2026-09-21 · **Decision this feeds:** the first non-Enigma model-selection experiment for the September 1941 Batch C corpus · **Out of scope:** claiming a cipher identification or plaintext from ciphertext-only summary statistics

## Facts

- CryptoCellar's current unbroken list names BYQMZ, FKQLZ, and XFEDT in September Batch C. QTXMA and SZAEJ are absent, but the accessible source does not explain their disposition. — [CryptoCellar unbroken-message list](https://www.cryptocellar.org/bgac/1941-msg-list-unbroken.html), status 2026-09-19; accessed 2026-09-21
- A declassified Friedman lecture identifies double transposition as another German Army cipher and describes its World War II cryptanalysis. — [The Friedman Lectures](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/lectures-speeches/FOLDER_166/41759739079888.pdf), declassified historical lecture; accessed 2026-09-21
- Friedman's treatment of periodic polyalphabetic systems explains causal repetitions when identical plaintext material is enciphered under the same cipher-alphabet positions, and contrasts these with random coincidences. — [Military Cryptanalysis, Part II](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/publications/FOLDER_508/41784299082338.pdf), 1938/1941 editions; accessed 2026-09-21
- The NSA's survey of German cipher machines documents Enigma, T-52 cipher teleprinters, and SZ-40/SZ-42 teleprinter attachments as German World War II systems. — [German Cipher Machines of World War II](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/about/cryptologic-heritage/historical-figures-publications/publications/wwii/german_cipher.pdf), 2014; accessed 2026-09-21
- The NSA NARA release catalog includes captured records titled “German Division Hand Key” and “German 3 Letter Code Tables,” both dated 1940. — [NSA World War II NARA Releases](https://www.nsa.gov/History/Cryptologic-History/NARA-Releases/), living catalog; accessed 2026-09-21
- The default 4,000-trial Phase 5 run routes QTXMA to the frequency-preserving/manual branch: IC 0.057729 with uniform-null upper-tail `p = 1/4001`. Its scan-corrected lag, candidate-period, and repeated-trigram tests also cross `alpha = 0.01`. The other four messages remain compatible with the uniform-random control under the configured tests. — [generated Phase 5 artifact](../artifacts/phase5-model-triage.json), 2026-09-21

## Inference

- A high monographic index of coincidence should route a message toward frequency-preserving systems, especially transposition, but cannot distinguish transposition from monoalphabetic substitution.
- A random-like monographic distribution does not favor Enigma over a cipher teleprinter or a strong superencipherment. Traffic format, procedure, cribs, or key recovery must do that work.
- Period scans and lag scans must be calibrated against the maximum statistic from each random trial; treating every tested period or lag as an independent uncorrected result would manufacture false positives.
- The five messages should be assessed separately before pooling. Phase 4 already showed that optimizing a shared model can overfit the visible messages and fail on held-out traffic.
- QTXMA is now the evidence-backed first target for a frequency-preserving family experiment. Its several positive tests are correlated structural observations, not four independent votes for a cipher name.

## Speculation

- QTXMA's visibly repetitive ciphertext may belong to a frequency-preserving manual system, a weak superencipherment, or a non-cryptographic/retransmission artifact. No source currently establishes which.
- The two trigrams on each form may be procedural carryover rather than proof that every body was generated by Enigma. This is a hypothesis to test, not a historical fact.

## Unresolved

- The original grouping, spacing, typography, and any operator corrections need to be re-extracted from the message-form facsimiles. Normalized A-Z strings discard evidence useful for code and transposition identification.
- The reason QTXMA and SZAEJ disappeared from the current unbroken list needs an archival or curator answer.
- Teleprinter-family plausibility cannot be ranked responsibly until the intercept medium and form-transcription path are documented.
- A frequency-preserving signal still requires a discriminating experiment: transposition search with held-out scoring, substitution controls, and exact preservation of message boundaries.

## Recommendation

Run deterministic, per-message structural triage first. Route any strong frequency-preserving outlier to a preregistered double-transposition experiment; route credible periodic outliers to repeating-key tests; leave random-like messages in a combined rotor/additive-machine branch until metadata can separate them.

Main risk: short ciphertexts create unstable tail statistics and can make an ordinary randomizing cipher look structured.

Strongest case against: the forms and indicator trigrams may already make standard Enigma so much more likely that alternative-family work has low expected value.

Reverse this if: the missing status history for QTXMA/SZAEJ reveals a confirmed solution or reclassification, or the facsimiles show that normalization created the apparent structure.
