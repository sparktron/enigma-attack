Set a daily key and watch an Enigma I encipher and decipher a message one key press at a time. Then attack the ciphertext the way this repository does: sweep the wheel start positions by index of coincidence, then hill-climb the plugboard with 1941 Army n-gram counts.

3Stage 1 · Sweep the wheel starts

The interceptor has only the ciphertext. The plugboard multiplies the keyspace beyond any search, so the method this repository follows (Weierud and Sullivan, with Ostwald and Weierud's two-phase scoring) first ignores it: decrypt with no plugs at every wheel setting and keep the settings whose output has the highest index of coincidence, the chance that two letters drawn from it match. German plaintext scores about 0.07 and random letters 0.0385.

This page sweeps all 17,576 starts of the sender's wheel order with the sender's rings held, one sixtieth of the 1,054,560 settings a Phase 1 sweep ranks per indicator ordering.

4Stage 2 · Climb the plugboard

At one wheel setting the climb tries every change of one plug: all 325 letter pairs and every unplug. It keeps the single best change and repeats until nothing improves. It climbs on index of coincidence first, then on the log-likelihood of the text under the published 1941 Army bigram and trigram counts, at most 10 pairs and 30 passes per phase, as phase1-stecker-calibration-v1 configures it.

    What the repository measured

    0%

    Chance the true setting reaches the top 200 of an IC-ranked sweep at 167 letters with ten plugs, over 32 key draws (phase1-ic-rank-v1). Stage 1 alone almost never finds it.

    28 / 32

    Trials in which a body-direct climb, IC phase then n-grams, recovered a ten-pair plugboard and the exact plaintext at 167 letters, on a plaintext of IC 0.079. On Army-typical text near 0.06 it recovers far fewer.

    2,109,120

    Daily keys in the complete indicator-coupled search of the real 1941 traffic. It found nothing, as the calibration predicted. BYQMZ, FKQLZ and XFEDT are still on the publisher's unbroken list.
